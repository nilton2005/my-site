In the sub-article [Who decides which folder each object goes in?](/en/journal/how-git-spreads-its-objects/) there was a sentence with fine print: "more even" means more even *in proportion*, not in count. Here we'll look at where that comes from, with the math and with graphs.

## The question

If you flip a coin 10 times, you might get 7 heads and 3 tails. If you flip it 10,000 times, you'll almost surely be very close to 50%. What changed? Did chance "make up for it" to fix the imbalance?

In the folders experiment something showed up that seems to contradict it:

| Objects | Difference between the fullest and the emptiest folder | Difference / average |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100,000 | 131 | 34% |
| 1,000,000 | 296 | 8% |

The difference **grew**, from 131 to 296 objects, but in proportion it **shrank**, from 34% to 8%. Both things are true at once, and the law of large numbers explains why.

## What we'll look at

- The statement of the law, without the fine print.
- A coin simulation, with graphs, to watch the proportion approach 50% while the absolute difference drifts away.
- The typical deviation, which for Git's folders is about √(n/256).
- How all of this applies to Git.
